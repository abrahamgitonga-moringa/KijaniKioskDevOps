Question 1: Risk of Running AI Analysis Before Manual Review
Risk Introduced: Running AI analysis first introduces anchoring bias. An engineer reading a plausible AI output is far more likely to accept the AI's premise without critically interrogating the raw log timestamps and metrics.

Plausible AI Misinterpretation: Generative AI models often associate scaling events (replica.added) with remediation and issue resolution. A plausible AI misinterpretation would claim: "At 02:12:19, the system automatically auto-scaled by adding a 4th replica, relieving load and resolving the database bottleneck."

Reality: The manual log analysis proves the exact opposite occurred. Adding a 4th replica created an additional application client competing for the same fixed poolSize: 20, spiking the waiting queue from 14 to 22 at 02:12:20Z and causing immediate payment failures (ORD-8874, ORD-8875). Relying on the AI first would have led an engineer to approve further horizontal scaling during an active incident, severely compounding database starvation.

Question 2: Why Removing the submitter Restriction in Jenkins is a Bad Idea
Security Principle (Least Privilege & Separation of Duties): Removing submitter violates the principle of Least Privilege and Non-Repudiation. In an un-restricted gate, an unprivileged account, compromised service account, or junior team member without production authorization can trigger a production deployment, bypassing organizational change control.

Audit Trail Failure (Accountability): Without submitter, any authenticated user (or automated bot token) can sign off. If an incident occurs immediately following deployment, the audit log (submitter: 'any') fails to establish clear human accountability, rendering the approval log useless for regulatory compliance and post-mortem analysis.

Question 3: Regulatory Compliance of Region Selection (us-east-1 vs. East Africa)
Data Governance Principle / Violation: Violates Data Sovereignty and Data Residency regulations (such as the Kenya Data Protection Act / East African financial data locality regulations), which mandate that sensitive personal customer and financial transaction data must be stored and processed within specified regional or national jurisdictions.

Required Terraform Change: Change region = "us-east-1" to region = "af-south-1" (AWS Cape Town) in the provider "aws" block.

Why it's a Compliance Requirement: Transmitting financial payment records outside the approved geopolitical boundary without explicit regulatory approval or user consent triggers severe legal penalties, potential loss of payment processor licenses, and immediate compliance audit failures for KijaniKiosk.

Question 4: Three Cross-Week Technical Dependencies
Week 10 (kk-receipts S3 Event Trigger) depends on Week 9 (Kubernetes kk-payments Pods):

Dependency: Week 10's generateReceipt handler receives incoming payment events generated by the Week 9 kk-payments deployment running in Kubernetes.

Failure Scenario: If the Week 9 kk-payments Kubernetes pods crash or fail their readiness probes (readinessProbe), no order payloads are emitted, leaving the Week 10 serverless receipt generation pipeline completely un-triggered.

Week 10 (Jenkins Approval Gate) depends on Week 5 (Jenkins Multi-Stage Pipeline & Docker Registry):

Dependency: The Week 10 Approve Production Deployment gate relies on the image tag artifact (kk-payments:${BUILD_NUMBER}) built and pushed during the Week 5 Docker build and registry push stages.

Failure Scenario: If the Week 5 Push to Registry stage fails or the image repository credentials break, the Week 10 Deploy to Production stage will fail during kubectl rollout status because the cluster cannot pull the target image tag.

Week 10 (Manifest sed Replacement) depends on Week 7 (Nginx & Declarative Kubernetes Manifests):

Dependency: The Week 10 pipeline updates k8s/kk-payments-deployment.yaml using sed before running kubectl apply -f.

Failure Scenario: If the Week 7 declarative manifest structure is missing or improperly formatted (e.g., missing the target image: string pattern), sed will silently fail to update the file, causing kubectl apply -f to redeploy the stale image tag and inadvertently rolling back production code.
